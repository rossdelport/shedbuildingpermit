Garages

Garage building permits in WA

Short answer

Yes, every garage in WA needs a building permit, attached or freestanding. Many also need planning approval if they are close to the street, wide across the frontage or built on a boundary. We work out which and handle both.

Last updated 29 October 2026

Garages always need a building permit

A garage is a building designed to house cars, with walls on all sides and usually a roller or panel door. In WA every garage needs a building permit, whether it is attached to the house or stands on its own. The permit checks the slab, the frame, the tie-downs and, if it is near a boundary or the house, fire separation.

A carport with a solid door added is treated as a garage, so it then has to meet the garage rules.

When a garage needs planning approval too

The R-Codes set standard rules for garages on house lots. A garage that meets them only needs a building permit. One that doesn't needs planning approval first. The common triggers are:

  • Too close to the street. Garages usually have to be set back further from the street than carports.
  • Too wide. Garage doors facing the street are limited to a share of the lot's frontage.
  • Boundary walls. A garage wall built on the side boundary has limits on its height and length.
  • Heritage areas and estates with their own design rules.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need a permit to convert a garage into a room?

Yes. Turning a garage into a bedroom, office or living space changes how the building is used, so it needs a building permit, and often planning approval too. It also has to meet the rules for habitable rooms, such as ceiling height, insulation, ventilation and light.

Can a garage be built on the boundary?

Often, yes, as a boundary wall within the R-Codes' height and length limits. Outside those limits it needs planning approval, and the wall has to meet fire-separation rules.

How long does a garage permit take?

Allow a week or so to prepare the documents, then up to 25 business days for a standard council decision. If planning approval is needed, that comes first and adds time.
