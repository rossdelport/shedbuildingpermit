How to get a shed permit in WA

Short answer

Check if your shed is exempt (10 m² or less and 2.4 m or lower), check your council's outbuilding rules, get plans and engineering, then lodge a BA2 with your council. It costs 0.32% of the value (at least $121) plus the levy, and council has up to 25 business days to decide.

Last updated 2 November 2026

Step 1: Check if you need a permit at all

A freestanding shed of 10 m² or less and no higher than 2.4 m doesn't need a building permit under the state's Building Regulations. It may still need planning approval if it breaks your council's rules, for example by sitting in the front setback. Anything bigger needs a building permit.

Step 2: Check the planning rules

Most councils let a shed skip planning approval if it meets the standard outbuilding rules. At the City of Wanneroo and the City of Melville, for example, that means:

  • no more than 60 m² or 10% of the lot (whichever is less)
  • walls no higher than 2.4 m and a ridge no higher than 4.2 m
  • at least 1 m from side and rear boundaries (Wanneroo asks for 1.5 m once a wall is longer than 14 m)
  • not used as a place to live, and not in the front setback

If your shed doesn't fit, you'll need a development application first. At most councils the fee is $147 for work up to $50,000. Use our shed size checker to test your numbers.

Step 3: Get drawings and engineering

Most shed kit suppliers provide plans and engineering certificates for your wind region. You'll also need a site plan (usually at least 1:200) showing the distances to every boundary and building, and elevations (usually at least 1:100).

Step 4: Lodge the application

Homeowners usually lodge a BA2 (uncertified) building permit application with their council. It costs 0.32% of the value of the work (at least $121), plus the $61.65 Building Services Levy for work up to $45,000. Some councils, like Wanneroo, also need a Street and Verge Bond application with every building permit. See how to fill in a BA2.

Step 5: Wait for the permit

Council has up to 25 business days to decide a BA2. If they ask for more information, the clock stops until you reply. Don't start building until the permit is issued.

Step 6: Build and finish

Build to the approved plans. Within 7 days of finishing, lodge a notice of completion (form BA7) with your council.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need a permit for a shed in WA?

Yes, unless it's freestanding, 10 m² or less and no higher than 2.4 m. Bigger sheds need a building permit, and some also need planning approval.

How long does a shed permit take in WA?

For a BA2 application, council has up to 25 business days. If it also needs planning approval, add that time first.

How much does a shed permit cost?

The council fee for a BA2 is 0.32% of the value, at least $121, plus the $61.65 Building Services Levy for work up to $45,000. Planning approval, if needed, is usually $147 for work up to $50,000.

Can I lodge a shed permit myself?

Yes. Homeowners can lodge a BA2 themselves with the plans and engineering from the shed supplier.
