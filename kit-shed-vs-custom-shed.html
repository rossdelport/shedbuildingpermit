Kit shed vs custom shed: who handles the permit?

Short answer

The permit rules are the same for kit and custom sheds: anything over 10 m² or 2.4 m high usually needs a building permit. The difference is paperwork. Kit makers may supply engineering and drawings, but you usually do the site plan and lodge it. With a custom shed, the builder often handles it, but check your contract.

Last updated 10 November 2026

The permit rules are the same

A council doesn't care whether your shed came in a kit or was designed from scratch. Both are Class 10a buildings, and both follow the same rules. The state has said there are no special exemptions for prefabricated or kit buildings, and warns that "permit-free" claims can be misleading.

A shed usually needs no building permit only if it's freestanding, 10 m² or less, no more than 2.4 m high, and not in wind region C or D. Anything bigger needs a permit, kit or not.

What councils ask for

The Shire of Serpentine-Jarrahdale's list is typical:

  • A BA1 or BA2 form, with all owners named
  • A site plan at 1:200 showing setbacks, shed size, soak wells or tanks, easements and any septic system
  • A floor plan and elevations at 1:100
  • Engineering signed by a structural engineer, covering footings, slab and tie-downs

The Town of Mosman Park says every shed needing a permit must be certified by a structural engineer, whichever form you use. Serpentine-Jarrahdale also says a second-hand shed needs a recent engineer's sign-off for the new site, and a shed within 6 m of a house in a bushfire-prone area may need a BAL assessment.

Do I need a registered builder?

From 1 July 2026, a Class 10a building such as a shed or garage under $50,000 doesn't need a registered builder, and an owner can build it without owner-builder approval. Over $50,000, the state says a prefab or kit building that needs a permit must be put up by a registered builder or an approved owner-builder.

Questions

Common questions

Does a kit shed need council approval in WA?

Yes, if it's over 10 m², over 2.4 m high, attached to another building or in wind region C or D. There are no special exemptions for kit or prefabricated buildings.

Does the kit shed company get the permit for me?

Some say they do, others only supply drawings and engineering. Ask the supplier in writing what's included before you buy.

Do I need an engineer for a kit shed?

Usually yes. Councils such as Mosman Park and Serpentine-Jarrahdale ask for engineering signed by a structural engineer. Some kit makers supply it for your wind rating.

Can I put up a second-hand kit shed?

Yes, with a permit if it's over the size limits. Serpentine-Jarrahdale asks for a recent engineer's sign-off for the new site.

Do I need a registered builder for a kit shed?

Not if it's under $50,000. From 1 July 2026 an owner can build a Class 10a shed under $50,000 without a registered builder or owner-builder approval.
