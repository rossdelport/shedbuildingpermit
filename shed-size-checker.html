Shed size checker: permit or no permit?

Short answer

In WA a shed needs a building permit if it is bigger than 10 m², taller than 2.4 m or attached to another building. It usually needs planning approval too if its walls are over 2.4 m, its roof is over 4.2 m, or all your outbuildings add up to more than 60 m² or 10% of the lot. Enter your sizes below to check.

The rules the checker uses

Two sets of rules decide what a shed needs. The checker applies the standard version of each.

Heights are measured from natural ground level. On a sloping block, or where the floor is raised, the shed can be taller than the kit says.

What the checker can't see

  • Your council's local policies. Some allow bigger sheds on large lots, or sheds closer to the fence.
  • Rural and special rural zones, which have their own outbuilding rules.
  • Heritage areas, estates and bushfire-prone lots, which can add requirements.
  • Sewer lines and easements that you can't build over.

If the checker says you need a permit, we can prepare and lodge it for you, including the site plan.

Questions

Common questions

What size shed can I build without a permit in WA?

A freestanding shed with a floor area of 10 m² or less and no taller than 2.4 m usually doesn't need a building permit. It still has to meet the planning rules for where it goes.

How big can a shed be on a residential lot in WA?

Under the standard R-Codes rules, all outbuildings together can be up to 60 m² or 10% of the lot, whichever is less, with walls up to 2.4 m and a roof up to 4.2 m. Bigger needs planning approval.

How do I measure the floor area of a shed?

Multiply the length by the width of the floor. A 3 m x 3.3 m shed is 9.9 m².

Does the checker work for rural properties?

Only roughly. Rural zones have their own outbuilding rules in each council's scheme, which often allow bigger sheds.
