How to get a shed approved in a bushfire-prone area

Short answer

In a bushfire-prone area, the key is the 6 m rule. A shed less than 6 m from your house usually needs a BAL assessment and bushfire construction if your house was built to bushfire rules. A shed 6 m or more away usually doesn't. It still needs a normal building permit.

Last updated 16 November 2026

Step 1: Check if you're in a bushfire-prone area

Land is mapped as bushfire-prone on the state's Map of Bush Fire Prone Areas, run by DFES. Look up your address on the map before you order a shed, because it can change what your permit needs.

Step 2: Check the 6 metre line

For bushfire purposes, the distance between the shed and your house matters most. Under the Building Regulations, when a shed, garage or other Class 10a building (or a deck) is less than 6 m from the house, the certificate that goes with the permit must say:

  • whether it's in a bushfire-prone area, and
  • if bushfire construction rules apply to it, the site's BAL (Bushfire Attack Level) or another approved assessment.

A shed 6 m or more from the house usually doesn't need to meet bushfire construction rules. Councils say the same thing in plain words: the Shire of Serpentine-Jarrahdale says a BAL may apply to a shed within 6 m of the house, and the City of Wanneroo says it applies if the house was built to bushfire rules and the shed is within 6 m.

Step 3: Check how your house was built

The regulations don't apply the bushfire rules to a shed or deck that goes with an older house that never had to meet bushfire construction rules. So the age and approval history of your house matters. Your building surveyor or council can confirm this from your property's records.

Step 4: Get a BAL assessment if you need one

If your shed is within 6 m of a house that was built to bushfire rules, you'll usually need a BAL assessment. The BAL tells your shed supplier which construction standard (AS 3959) the shed must meet, such as ember screens or sealed gaps.

Step 5: Lodge the building permit

The rest is the same as any shed: the BA1 or BA2 form, a site plan showing the distance to the house, elevations, a floor plan and engineering. Show the distance to the house clearly on the site plan.

Not in a bushfire-prone areaNo
Bushfire-prone, shed 6 m or more from the houseUsually no
Bushfire-prone, within 6 m, house built to bushfire rulesUsually yes: BAL needed

Questions

Common questions

Does a shed need a BAL in WA?

Usually only if it's in a bushfire-prone area and less than 6 m from a house that was built to bushfire rules. A shed 6 m or more away usually doesn't.

How far should a shed be from the house in a bushfire area?

6 m is the key distance. Closer than that, the permit certificate must state whether it's bushfire-prone and, where the rules apply, the BAL.

How do I know if my property is bushfire-prone?

Look up your address on the state's Map of Bush Fire Prone Areas.

Does a small exempt shed need to meet bushfire rules?

A shed that's exempt from a building permit (freestanding, up to 10 m² and 2.4 m) doesn't go through the permit process, but it still has to meet the building standards that apply to it. Ask your council if you're close to the house.
