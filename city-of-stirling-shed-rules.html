City of Stirling shed rules

Short answer

In Stirling, a shed needs no planning approval if it's one shed up to 10 m², or sheds totalling up to 60 m² (or 10% of the lot) with 2.4 m walls set 1 m off the boundary. Heritage areas always need approval. A building permit is needed over 10 m² or 2.4 m high.

Last updated 22 November 2026

Heritage areas always need approval

Every shed in the Mount Lawley, Menora and Inglewood Heritage Protection Area, or on a heritage-listed property, needs planning approval and must follow the City's character guidelines (LPP 3.1).

Do you need a building permit?

Not for a freestanding shed of 10 m² or less and 2.4 m or less high (outside cyclone wind regions). Anything bigger needs a building permit.

The City's own example: a 27 m² shed on a 220 m² R40 lot is over 10% of the lot, so it needs both planning approval and a building permit.

If you need planning approval

  • An application form signed by every owner, plus the fee.
  • A certificate of title issued in the last 6 months.
  • A 1:200 site plan, plus 1:100 floor plans and elevations showing heights and ground levels.

You don't need your neighbour's approval to apply. The City has 60 days to decide, but sheds are usually faster. Strata lots may also need strata approval. Call the City on 9205 8555.

Questions

Common questions

Do I need council approval for a shed in Stirling?

Not planning approval if it fits the City's small shed or large shed rules and isn't in a heritage area. A building permit is needed if it's over 10 m² or over 2.4 m high.

How big can a shed be in Stirling without planning approval?

Up to 60 m² in total, or 10% of your lot, whichever is less, with walls up to 2.4 m and a ridge up to 4.2 m.

Can I put a shed on the boundary in Stirling?

A single shed up to 10 m² and 2.7 m high can sit on up to two boundaries. Larger sheds need to be 1 m off, or 1.5 m for walls over 14 m long.

Do I need my neighbour's permission for a shed in Stirling?

No. You don't need your neighbour's approval to lodge a planning application, though the City may ask them for comment.
