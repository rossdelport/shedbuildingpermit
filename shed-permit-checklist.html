Shed permit checklist builder

Short answer

Most WA councils want a site plan, plans and elevations, and a structural engineer's certification for a shed. You may also need a BA3, a BAL assessment, owner-builder approval or a verge permit, depending on your shed.

What most councils ask for

For an uncertified shed application, the City of Swan's checklist asks for a site plan, working drawings with a cross section, structural engineer certification, and numbered documents. The Shire of Serpentine Jarrahdale asks for soak wells and easements on the site plan, and engineer-signed footing, slab and tie-down details.

Extra items that catch people out

  • Second-hand sheds: Serpentine Jarrahdale needs a recent engineer's sign-off.
  • Bushfire areas: a shed within 6 m of the house may need to meet the house's BAL rating.
  • Bigger jobs: over $50,000 you need a registered builder or owner-builder approval.
  • Breaking a rule: if the shed doesn't meet the R-Codes' standard rules, you may need neighbour comments or planning approval first.

Questions

Common questions

What documents do I need for a shed permit in WA?

Usually the application form, a site plan, plans and elevations, and a structural engineer's certification. Some sheds need more, such as a BAL assessment or owner-builder approval.

Does every shed need engineering?

Most councils ask for an engineer's certification of the footings, slab and tie-down. Shed kits usually come with it.

Can I use a second-hand shed?

Usually yes, but some councils, such as Serpentine Jarrahdale, need a recent engineer's sign-off that it's fit to re-use.

Do I need a BA3 for a shed?

Only if you lodge a BA1. With a BA2, the council checks the plans itself.
