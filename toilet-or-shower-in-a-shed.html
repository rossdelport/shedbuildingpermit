Question answer

Can I put a toilet or shower in my shed?

Short answer

Usually yes. A licensed plumber must do the work and give you a certificate of compliance, and the waste must go to the sewer or an approved septic system. The shed must stay a shed: if it has a bathroom, kitchen and bed, council treats it as a dwelling, which needs different approvals.

Last updated 31 October 2026

When it stops being a shed

A toilet, basin or shower on its own is common in workshops, man caves and pool sheds. The trouble starts when a shed has a toilet, a shower and a kitchen and bed. At that point it looks like somewhere to live, and councils assess it as a dwelling, such as a granny flat. That means a different building standard (insulation, ceiling heights, energy efficiency, fire separation) and possibly planning approval.

The planning exemptions for sheds also depend on this. The City of Melville, for example, says a shed in a residential zone usually skips planning approval only if, among other things, it is non-habitable. If you want somewhere to live, build it as a granny flat from the start.

The approvals for a shed with a bathroom

  • Building permit. Most sheds need one anyway once they are over 10 m² or 2.4 m high. Show the toilet, shower and any wall linings on the plans.
  • Plumbing. Your plumber handles the plumbing paperwork. For new fixtures, they must give you a certificate of compliance within five working days of finishing. Keep it, as buyers and insurers may ask for it.
  • Septic. If your lot isn't on sewer, adding a toilet means a septic or treatment system, which needs council's health approval before it is installed.
  • Electrical. Hot water, lighting and an exhaust fan need a licensed electrician.

Practical tips

  • Order a drainage plumbing diagram for your property before you pick the shed's spot. It shows where your sewer runs, so you can place the shed where connecting is cheapest.
  • Don't build over existing sewer or drainage pipes.
  • Remember the shed setbacks: usually 1 m from side and rear boundaries to skip planning approval. How close can a shed be to the fence?

Questions

Common questions

Do I need a permit to put a toilet in my shed in WA?

The plumbing must be done by a licensed plumber, who gives you a certificate of compliance. The shed itself needs a building permit if it is over 10 m² or 2.4 m high, and the toilet should be shown on the plans.

Can I put a shower in my shed?

Yes, as long as a licensed plumber installs it and the shed is not used as somewhere to live. A shed with a bathroom, kitchen and bed is treated as a dwelling.

Can I live in my shed if it has a bathroom?

No. A shed is an outbuilding that is not lived in. To live in it, it must be approved as a dwelling, such as a granny flat, which has stricter building rules.

Can I do the plumbing in my shed myself?

No. WA homeowners can only do a few basic tasks, like replacing a shower head or a cistern washer. All other water supply, sanitary and drainage plumbing must be done by a licensed plumber.
